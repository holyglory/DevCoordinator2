# User Issue Ledger: UI

| ID | Applies to | Mistake pattern | Required behavior | Prevention and verification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UIL-UI-001 | Product UI implementation and completion claims | Agents delivered visible enabled controls that were inert, simulated, only partially wired, or verified by routes, rendering, screenshots, or geometry without exercising the promised interaction. | Every visible enabled control must perform its stated action end to end and produce the expected observable result through the rendered interface. Mock-data prototypes may use synthetic data, but their visible interactions must work truthfully within the declared prototype boundary. Unimplemented agreed behavior remains an explicit, control-specific item in the authoritative software-owned database completion ledger and blocks completion; a specification-required future control is semantically disabled, visibly unavailable, and tracked there, while out-of-scope future information is noninteractive content rather than a control. | Before UI completion, inventory every visible interactive element across only the agreed screens, journeys, states, and responsive variants; map it to its journey and observable result; exercise applicable success, cancellation, validation, permission-failure, recovery, and persistence paths; batch-record and fix gaps; and require zero behaviorless enabled controls, zero requested journeys without rendered end-to-end evidence, and zero request-related completion-ledger entries. Enforce direct skip, representative-subset, catch-all-ledger, clickable-future-control, and static-sufficiency must-catch variants plus negative-phrasing precision guards, without implicitly authorizing a broader audit. |
| UIL-UI-002 | Headings, labels, cards, settings, and their supporting copy | Agents added subtitles, helper text, or descriptive copy by default, often restating a clear heading or label and making the interface verbose. | Prefer one concise, self-explanatory heading or label. Add supporting copy only when the user explicitly requests it or it is necessary to prevent misunderstanding or error; never use it to restate the heading or label. | Review subordinate UI copy across the agreed screens and variants; remove default or redundant descriptions while preserving necessary instructions and warnings. Enforce must-catch fixtures for prefix/default/blanket copy, discretionary “helpful” descriptions, and restatement by repeat, paraphrase, or echo; independently guard explicit-request, error-prevention, and negative-quantifier phrasing from false positives. |
| UIL-UI-003 | Data-backed UI content and completion claims | Agents inserted made-up numbers, parameters, statuses, or results so a product screen looked populated, then left the real data source, processing, or persistence unfinished and untracked. | Production UI presents factual values only from a real source, user input, measurement, imported data, or an explicitly requested deterministic definition. If agreed data is unavailable, show an honest loading, error, empty, or unavailable state and keep the missing integration in the authoritative software-owned database completion ledger; do not substitute plausible demo values. Synthetic content remains isolated to an explicitly declared design, test, or mock-data prototype and is never presented as production behavior. | Enforce truthful-data policy terms and must-catch fixtures for plausible invented metrics, parameters, and results plus an unledgered missing integration. Preserve passing fixtures for clearly declared test/design examples and honest unavailable states. Verify the rendered production path against its real data source and require no request-related ledger item before completion. |
| UIL-UI-004 | Destination layout and first-viewport hierarchy | Agents opened collection or task destinations with a rare create/configuration form before the object or task named by the destination, especially on narrow screens. | A destination's primary journey content must be the first substantial recognizable content in the initial viewport. Compact supporting controls and a necessary blocking alert may precede it, but a lower-frequency form, configuration surface, or secondary journey must not displace it. | Require an explicit primary journey and rendered region contract per route/state; measure the initial viewport before scrolling; include must-catch fixtures for create forms or secondary workflows above collections and false-positive guards for dedicated create pages, empty states, compact toolbars, and blocking alerts. |
| UIL-UI-005 | In-page journey continuation after activating a control | Agents wired a visible action to reveal its next form or panel below a long page, leaving users unaware that anything happened or forcing them to hunt by scrolling. | An in-page action must reveal a visible continuation anchor in the current viewport, move focus into the revealed surface, and avoid a disorienting document jump. A deliberate modal, narrow-screen sheet, nearby expansion, or expected destination route may satisfy the continuation when its primary anchor is immediately visible. | Capture pre-action scroll and post-action focus/geometry; fail offscreen, unfocused, or unexpectedly scrolled continuation surfaces. Exercise modal, sheet, nearby expansion, dedicated navigation, and long-list cases through the rendered interface. |
| UIL-UI-006 | Theme, contrast, palette, and visual evidence | Agents treated a layout-only pass or one screenshot as sufficient even when text blended into its background, a declared dark/light theme was dominated by opposite-brightness surfaces, or a visually incoherent palette needed judgment. | Deterministic checks block measurable WCAG contrast failures and major contradictions of the declared light/dark theme. Palette composition that cannot be judged truthfully by fixed thresholds remains explicit review evidence, never an invented automatic aesthetic verdict. Every checked route/state/viewport retains both its first-viewport and full-page captures. | Test readable and unreadable text, light/dark/mixed themes, small accents, gradients, charts, media, and competing prominent hues. Bind every capture to its exact cell and integrity hash, report unmeasurable surfaces, and require agent review for queued palette risks. |
| UIL-UI-007 | Repeated manual review of visual evidence | Agents interpreted screenshot pixel drift as a UI change and manually reviewed dynamic pages repeatedly even when their UI implementation and journey intent were unchanged. | Manual re-review is triggered only by changed declared UI code, styles, tokens, fonts, assets, journey/theme intent, or a newly covered route/state/viewport. Screenshot hashes prove evidence integrity only; they never trigger review. An unchanged prior gap remains blocking without reopening the same images. | Require per-target implementation-input fingerprints and an explicitly supplied prior reviewed manifest. Prove dynamic pixel changes and unrelated backend/test changes do not enter the review queue, while mapped UI-input changes, intent changes, and new cells do; reject raw unreviewed baselines and carry unchanged gaps forward. |
| UIL-UI-008 | Scrollbars and same-direction scroll nesting | Agents treated active scrollbars as neutral inventory, allowing unexpected horizontal scrolling and confusing layers of same-direction scrollable panels to reach users. | Every active horizontal scrollbar is exceptional and produces a warning; a horizontal scrollbar inside another horizontal scroll path is a blocker. Two active vertical layers produce a warning and three or more are blockers. The document scrollbar counts as a layer, while differently directed ancestors do not increase same-axis depth. | Record every active scrollbar with its outer-to-inner same-axis chain and depth. Use realistic browser fixtures for single and forced horizontal bars, nested horizontal failure, double and triple vertical nesting, a document-plus-panel chain, mixed-axis precision, and non-overflowing `auto` containers. |
